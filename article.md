# Humanoids Grew Up This Fall. Three Things Changed.

*From Shanghai to Savannah to San Francisco, the last ten weeks showed where humanoid robotics is going: into public markets, into human hands, and into the ring.*

---

> **TL;DR**
> - **The money got real.** Unitree went public at a ~$50B valuation, then lost almost half of it. Agility is weeks from Nasdaq. Investors now want revenue, not demos.
> - **The human in the loop became the product.** Human training data now has its own market, with $100M+ revenue run-rates. Even the most advanced robots still need a person in the loop for their hardest jobs.
> - **Robot combat went mainstream, and got a referee.** Over 2,000 robots competed in Beijing, Shenzhen launched a pro league, and California stepped in. The sport is now a proving ground for the whole industry.

---

## 1. The money got real

![Unitree celebrates its Shanghai STAR Market debut](https://newsstatic.rthk.hk/images/mfile_1866694_1_M_20260819114028.jpg)
*Unitree's Shanghai STAR Market listing, Aug 19, 2026. Photo: [RTHK](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1866694-20260819.htm)*

On **August 19**, Unitree listed on Shanghai's STAR Market at 150.8 yuan a share. It closed its first day at 845 yuan, up about 460%, which made it a **~$50 billion company** [[1]](#sources). Within a week the stock had fallen roughly 45% from its peak [[2]](#sources). By September, Chinese regulators were reportedly telling banks to raise the bar for humanoid IPOs and asking for **recurring revenue and narrowing losses** [[3]](#sources).

The same shift is visible elsewhere:

- **China:** UBTech's first-half revenue doubled to RMB 1.27B. It sold **921 full-size humanoids**, roughly 20 times as many as a year earlier, and its net loss narrowed 23% [[4]](#sources). AgiBot has started its Hong Kong IPO process [[5]](#sources). On October 13, Shanghai set a target of **8,000 humanoids working in its factories by 2028** [[6]](#sources).
- **US:** On October 13 the SEC declared Agility Robotics' merger filing effective. Shareholders vote on **November 2**. If the deal closes, Agility lists on Nasdaq as AGLT with over $620M in new capital, a $2.5B pre-money value, and more than **$300M in committed Digit orders** [[7]](#sources). In Georgia, Hyundai and Boston Dynamics opened a center to train Atlas for factory work, with a plan to deploy **25,000 Atlas units** across Hyundai and Kia plants [[8]](#sources).
- **Europe:** London-based Humanoid raised **$152M at a $1.35B valuation** in July [[9]](#sources).

Globally, about **25,000 humanoids shipped in the first half of 2026**, up 432% year over year, according to IDC [[10]](#sources). That is IDC's figure; Counterpoint puts the same period at just over 22,000 [[11]](#sources).

**The takeaway:** the market is no longer asking *whether* humanoids will ship. It is asking *who can make them earn*.

## 2. The human in the loop became the product

The most advanced humanoid hands in the world still need a person behind them for the hardest jobs.

[![New Hands for Atlas | Boston Dynamics](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/4whgw2gLBS8/hqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4whgw2gLBS8)
*▶ Watch: "New Hands for Atlas", Boston Dynamics, Oct 1, 2026*

On October 1, Boston Dynamics unveiled a new Atlas hand: **13 degrees of freedom, up from 7**, with dense tactile sensing, built to handle drills, nail guns and welding torches [[12]](#sources). Coverage of the launch video noted that the drill demo was run with **a remote operator**, while a simpler two-ball manipulation was autonomous [[13]](#sources).

That pattern shows up across the industry. Human skill is no longer only a stopgap until autonomy arrives. Companies are now selling it, as data and as a feature:

| Company | What they sell | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| **Mecka** (US/Canada) | First-person recordings of people doing real tasks, used to train robots | **$60M Series B led by Sequoia** (Oct 7), with NVIDIA, Microsoft's M12, Qualcomm and Samsung. Says it passed a **$100M revenue run-rate** in June [[14]](#sources) |
| **XDOF** (US) | Teleoperation data plus sensor-wearing human collectors | In talks to raise at a **~$1.2B valuation**, three months after leaving stealth, with annualized revenue nearing $50M [[15]](#sources) |
| **Microagi** (Germany) | Human data captured with head cameras and sensor gloves for factory robots | **$55M seed** (July) [[16]](#sources) |
| **1X** (US/Norway) | NEO home humanoid with "Expert Mode": a remote 1X operator handles tasks the robot cannot yet do alone | Selling at $20,000 or $499/month [[17]](#sources) |
| **HTX** (Singapore) | Government humanoid training center for HazMat, post-blast and emergency work | Dedicated **motion-capture, teleoperation and human-demonstration** zones; robots alongside officers "as soon as 2028" [[18]](#sources) |

![XDOF data collection setup](https://techcrunch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/DSC_2479.jpg?resize=1200,800)
*Teleoperation data collection at XDOF. Photo: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/17/collecting-robot-training-data-is-dirty-unglamorous-work-some-ai-labs-are-already-paying-xdof-to-do-it/)*

> "Motion, contact, force and geometry aren't on the internet. You can't scrape them, license them or buy them."
> (Mecka [[14]](#sources))

**The takeaway:** language models learned from text people had already written down. Robots have to learn from human movement that nobody has recorded yet, and it is now a paid market. The open question is **which human data is worth the most**: calm, everyday motion, or fast, forceful, high-pressure motion that is far harder to capture.

## 3. Robot combat went mainstream, and got a referee

![Robots competing at the 2026 World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing](https://asianews.network/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/6a89ad6aa3106bc5df9271b3.jpeg)
*World Humanoid Robot Games, Beijing, August 2026. Photo: [China Daily / Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/world-humanoid-robot-games-open-in-beijing/)*

If you want to know whether a humanoid is ready for the real world, the clearest test right now may be watching it fight.

- **Beijing (Aug 22–26):** The second World Humanoid Robot Games drew **666 teams and 2,056 robots from 16 countries**, roughly four times last year's field [[19]](#sources).
- **Shenzhen:** EngineAI launched **URKL**, a commercial humanoid fighting league with a **10-million-yuan prize belt**. Its opening night ended with a robot's head kicked clean off [[20]](#sources). Finals run through December.
- **Hangzhou (Sept 7):** Unitree released footage of a G1 sparring with a human **with no teleoperator**, which it calls the first fully autonomous humanoid combat [[21]](#sources). Independent verification is still pending.
- **San Francisco (Sept 18):** The league REK put an influencer in the cage against a **VR-piloted** EngineAI T800. Within days, the **California State Athletic Commission** ordered a stop to unsanctioned human-vs-robot bouts [[22]](#sources).

[![World's First URKL Robot Combat – Opening Night](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/vpyO73jyx1g/hqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vpyO73jyx1g)
*▶ Watch: URKL opening night in Shenzhen (via Shenzhen Story)*

[![Unitree: Fully Autonomous Humanoid Robot Combat](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/qkIJELDgULA/hqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qkIJELDgULA)
*▶ Watch: Unitree's autonomous combat demo*

The format that gets the most out of the machine without putting people in harm's way is **robot vs. robot, with elite humans piloting**. On **October 7** in Singapore, ARC Night staged the city's first full-sized humanoid fight night. Muay Thai, MMA, boxing and kung fu athletes, including national and international champions, wore **full-body motion-capture suits** and piloted Unitree G1 and EngineAI T800 humanoids in real time, in front of 380+ people [[23]](#sources). In that format the athlete supplies the intent, the robot supplies balance and execution, and the only thing that gets hit is hardware.

![An ARC pilot and their humanoid avatar in matching guard](https://arcleague.com/arc/silver/ring-stand.webp)
*ARC Night: the pilot on the outside, the avatar mirroring every move inside the cage.*

**The takeaway:** combat brings together all three trends in this piece. It is the most punishing public test bench for humanoid hardware. It produces the hardest kind of human motion data: fast, contact-heavy and decided under pressure. And it is building an audience for the industry before most robots ever reach a factory floor.

## What to watch next

1. **Oct 21: Tesla's Q3 call.** This is the first chance for a real Optimus production number since Fremont was converted [[24]](#sources).
2. **Nov 2: Agility's shareholder vote.** If it passes, the US gets its first publicly listed pure-play humanoid company [[7]](#sources).
3. **The fight calendar.** URKL's finals run through December, more ARC nights are planned after Singapore, and regulators are deciding where the line sits between sport and safety [[20]](#sources)[[22]](#sources)[[23]](#sources).

The race to build humanoid bodies is crowded and well funded. The race to give those bodies **human experience**, in factories, in emergencies and in the ring, has only just started.
