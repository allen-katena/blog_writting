### 1/

Humanoid robotics grew up this fall.

Six weeks of news from Shanghai, Savannah, Zhuhai and Singapore point to three shifts:

1. Money now follows revenue
2. Human skill is becoming a product
3. Entertainment is where people already pay to see humanoids

🧵

📷 assets/images/arc-night-singapore.jpg

---

### 2/

1. Money now follows revenue

China's securities regulator reportedly told banks to raise the bar for humanoid IPOs. Unitree, the first mainland listing, was down ~55% from its peak.

Companies waiting to list now need recurring revenue and narrowing losses, not just demos.

---

### 3/

The money is still there. It's going to companies with real deployments:

• Agility: if shareholders approve Nov 2, it lists on Nasdaq with $300M+ in committed orders
• Hyundai plans 25,000 Atlas units. Boston Dynamics named ex-Amazon AGI head Rohit Prasad CEO

📷 assets/images/atlas-rmac-georgia.jpg

---

### 4/

• Tesla is reportedly building several hundred Optimus a week, ~10x Q2
• AGIBOT rolled out its 20,000th robot
• Shanghai wants 8,000 humanoids in its factories by 2028

IDC: ~25,000 shipped in H1 2026 (+432% YoY), 750,000/yr by 2030.

Now: who can make them pay?

---

### 5/

2. Human skill is becoming a product

Boston Dynamics' new Atlas hand has 13 degrees of freedom, up from 7, built for drills and welding torches.

But in the launch video, the drill demo was run by a remote operator. Only a simpler task was autonomous.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4whgw2gLBS8

---

### 6/

Across the industry, human skill is now sold directly:

• Mecka records people doing real tasks and sells the data to robot labs. $60M Series B led by Sequoia, $100M+ revenue run-rate
• XDOF sells teleoperation data. In talks at ~$1.2B, 3 months out of stealth

🎬 assets/videos/mecka-series-b.mp4

---

### 7/

Others build it into the product:

• Minerva's "Roger" lets specialists do bomb disposal remotely in VR. ~$10M pre-seed led by General Catalyst
• Singapore's HTX opened a center to train humanoids for emergencies using motion capture and teleoperation

🎥 Video: @iamwillcannon

🎬 assets/videos/minerva-roger_will-cannon.mp4

---

### 8/

Language models learned from text people had already written.

Robots have to learn from human movement that was never recorded, and that data is now a paid market.

Calm, everyday motion is easy to capture. Fast, forceful decisions under pressure are harder, and worth more.

---

### 9/

3. Entertainment is where people already pay to see humanoids

Factories are the long-term prize. But audiences are buying tickets today.

AGIBOT and Chimelong opened a theme park in Zhuhai with 300+ humanoid robots in its first phase.

📷 assets/images/agibot-chimelong.jpg

---

### 10/

The robot makers are building the shows themselves.

EngineAI runs its own fight league, URKL: 32 teams fight on company-supplied T800s for a gold belt worth ~$1.4M. Finals are set for Nov–Dec.

Every bout is a public stress test of its own product.

---

### 11/

Unitree is chasing the same stage.

It released footage of a G1 sparring a human with no teleoperator, which it calls the first fully autonomous humanoid combat (not independently verified).

It isn't a product. It shows how fast the tech behind these shows is moving.

🎬 assets/videos/unitree-autonomous-combat.mp4

---

### 12/

What made ARC Night (Oct 7) special:

• Singapore's first full-sized humanoid fight night
• Every bout teleoperated in real time

Muay Thai, MMA and boxing champions in mocap suits piloted humanoids move for move for 380+ people. Only machines took the hits.

---

### 13/

Live entertainment ties all three shifts together:

• Audiences pay now, while factory fleets ramp
• It's the toughest public test of humanoid hardware
• It produces the hardest human data to collect: fast, contact-heavy decisions under pressure

---

### 14/

What to watch next:

• Nov 2: Agility's vote. If it passes, the US gets its first listed pure-play humanoid company
• URKL finals (Nov–Dec): can a robot maker turn combat into a lasting format?

---

### 15/

Building humanoid bodies is now a crowded, well-funded race.

Teaching those bodies human skill, in factories, in emergencies and in front of a crowd, is still early. That's where the opportunity is.
